      <span class="publication-main">
        <span class="publication-title">Principal specializations of Grothendieck polynomials</span>
        <span class="publication-authors">Haojun Bai, Feng Gu, Peter L. Guo, and Jiaji Liu</span>
      </span>
      <span class="publication-actions" aria-label="Publication actions">
        <button type="button" class="publication-action publication-abstract-toggle" aria-expanded="false">Abstract</button>
        <a class="publication-action publication-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10276" target="_blank" rel="noopener">arXiv</a>
      </span>
    </div>
    <div class="publication-abstract" hidden>
      <p>
        Motivated by Stanley's ''Schubert shenanigans'' paper,  commendable attempts have been made to understand the principal specializations of Schubert or Grothendieck polynomials. In this paper, we prove that the principal specialization of the    $\beta$-Grothendieck polynomial $\mathfrak{G}_w^{(\beta)}(x)$ can be expressed nonnegatively in terms of the occurrences of patterns in the permutation  $w$  when  $w$ does not contain  the $1423$ pattern.   Using  an inverse conservation principle, we further obtain  the nonnegativity expansion for permutations avoiding the $1342$ pattern. Our results  partially resolve   conjectures raised  respectively by Gao,  Me\'sz\'aros--Tanjaya, and Dennin. The proofs are achieved  based upon a reduction algorithm performing on  the   pipe dream model of $\beta$-Grothendieck polynomials. 
      </p>

        <span class="publication-title">$K$-$k$-Schur functions are $k$-Schur positive</span>
        <span class="publication-authors">Haojun Bai,  Peter L. Guo</span>
      </span>
      <span class="publication-actions" aria-label="Publication actions">
        <button type="button" class="publication-action publication-abstract-toggle" aria-expanded="false">Abstract</button>
        <a class="publication-action publication-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01708" target="_blank" rel="noopener">arXiv</a>
      </span>
    </div>
    <div class="publication-abstract" hidden>
      <p>
       The $k$-Schur functions (resp., $K$-$k$-Schur functions) are the symmetric function  representatives of Schubert classes in the homology (resp., $K$-homology) of the affine Grassmannian of type $A$. We prove  that every $K$-$k$-Schur function is a nonnegative integer linear combination in the basis of  $k$-Schur functions. This settles   a conjecture  by Lam, Schilling  and Shimozono. The proof relies  on realizations of $k$-Schur   and $K$-$k$-Schur functions as    Catalan and  Katalan functions, respectively. 
      </p>

      <span class="publication-main">
        <span class="publication-title">Monk and Pieri rules for forest polynomials</span>
        <span class="publication-authors">Haojun Bai, Shu Xiao Li, and David G.L. Wang</span>
      </span>
      <span class="publication-actions" aria-label="Publication actions">
        <button type="button" class="publication-action publication-abstract-toggle" aria-expanded="false">Abstract</button>
        <a class="publication-action publication-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11321" target="_blank" rel="noopener">arXiv</a>
      </span>
    </div>
    <div class="publication-abstract" hidden>
      <p>
       This article gives Pieri rules for forest polynomials and Monk rule for double forest polynomials. We construct binary relations $\lessdot_i$ and $\prec_i$ based on the local Tamari moves on indexed forests. These relations are explicit and constructive. For ordinary forest polynomials, the $h$- and $e$-Pieri rules are given by all chains of a fixed length along the cover relations, with certain restriction on the left or right endpoints. For double forest polynomials, our Monk rule shows every off-diagonal degree-one structure coefficient is always a single positive root, whose weight is also controlled by Tamari moves. Additionally, we introduce an insertion algorithm on positive injective words that yields a Monk rule for the corresponding slide polynomials. Regrouping this rule along Sylvester classes gives an independent Monk rule for ordinary forest polynomials.
      </p>
